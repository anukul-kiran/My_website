How I decide an agent is ready

An agent gives a slightly different answer every time you ask, so one passing test doesn't tell you much. Before a change reaches pilot users, I check it against two questions.

Does it match the data?

The answer has to agree with what the underlying data says for the question that was asked. No invented figures, no confident guesses.

Can a marketer act on it?

A suggestion can be accurate and still useless. It has to point to a next step someone on a campaign team would take.

Pilot marketers tell us where the agents fall short, and I turn that into prioritised requirements for the next release.

The suite works across Adobe Journey Optimizer, Adobe Experience Platform, Marketo Engage and Pega Customer Decision Hub.

Research

From December 2024 to August 2025 I was a junior research fellow at SRM Institute of Science and Technology, on a DRDO-funded programme for classifying objects in underwater sonar, working with the Department of Ocean Engineering at IIT Madras.

Reading the shadow as well as the highlight

In side-scan sonar, an object shows up twice. There's a bright return where sound bounces off it, and a dark acoustic shadow behind it where sound can't reach. The shadow often says more about an object's shape, but it's also the part that noise destroys first. Our model reads both regions and learns, image by image, how much weight to give each.

Classification accuracy on real sonar images, from our OCEANS 2025 paper
Context-adaptive fusion (our model)96.75%
Shadow and highlight classifier92.18%
Shadow-only classifier85.98%

We also built two things the model needed:

  • A denoising model that keeps shadows intact: a U-Net with squeeze-and-excitation blocks and a Grad-CAM-guided loss. It had the best structural similarity (SSIM 0.64) of the seven methods we compared.
  • S3Simulator+, a benchmark of 7,000 to 8,000 simulated sonar images of ships, planes and naval mines, with physics-informed noise for multipath interference, backscatter and reverberation.
